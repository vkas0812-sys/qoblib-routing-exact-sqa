\begin{abstract}
The Capacitated Vehicle Routing Problem (CVRP) benchmark class (\texttt{09-routing}) in the Quantum Optimization Benchmark Library (QOBLIB) consists of 55 instances (\texttt{XSH-n20-k4-01} to \texttt{XSH-n20-k4-55}) with $n=20$ customers and $K=4$ vehicles. Despite their small customer count, standard Mixed-Integer Programming (MIP) and Quadratic Unconstrained Binary Optimization (QUBO) formulations struggle to close the optimality gap within a 1-hour limit for 54 out of 55 instances due to weak linear relaxations and severe quadratic penalty barriers. In this technical note, we show that every instance in \texttt{09-routing} possesses an exact \emph{zero-slack tight-capacity invariant}: $\sum_{i=1}^{20} d_i = 4Q$. Consequently, the feasible solution manifold collapses from $O(K^n)$ assignments to between $300$ and $961$ single-vehicle subsets and between $372$ and $5,630$ valid 4-vehicle partitions per instance. Exploiting this invariant, we introduce: (1) \textbf{TC-Exact}, a deterministic bitwise exact-cover and Held-Karp dynamic programming solver that certifies global optimality (\texttt{proven\_optimal = TRUE}) across all \textbf{55/55 instances} in an average of \textbf{3.83 ms} per instance (including certifying $\text{Cost}=842$ on the hardest instance \texttt{XSH-n20-k4-21}, where the second-best partition has $\text{Cost}=843$); and (2) \textbf{SQA-Subspace}, a Path-Integral Simulated Quantum Annealing algorithm evolving $P=16$ Trotter replicas directly on the demand-tight partition manifold via multi-route recombination tunneling, achieving \textbf{55/55 (100\%) global optimality} in \textbf{22.4 ms} per run compared to \textbf{0/55} for penalty-based QUBO/Ising SQA.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and the Penalty Barrier in QOBLIB \texttt{09-routing}}

The Quantum Optimization Benchmark Library (QOBLIB)~\cite{koch2025qoblib} establishes standardized, verifiable benchmark challenges across ten combinatorial optimization classes to evaluate classical, quantum-inspired, and quantum hardware solvers. Class \texttt{09-routing} focuses on the Capacitated Vehicle Routing Problem (CVRP) with 55 instances (\texttt{XSH-n20-k4-01} through \texttt{XSH-n20-k4-55}), each comprising $n=20$ customers, a central depot (node $0$), $K=4$ identical vehicles of capacity $Q \in \{35, 1000\}$, and rounded 2D Euclidean distances (\texttt{EUC\_2D}):
\begin{equation}
  c_{uv} = \lfloor \sqrt{(x_u - x_v)^2 + (y_u - y_v)^2} + 0.5 \rfloor, \quad \forall u, v \in \{0, 1, \dots, n\}.
\end{equation}

In standard QUBO/Ising formulations of CVRP, binary variables $x_{i,r,t} \in \{0,1\}$ encode whether customer $i$ is visited at position $t$ by vehicle $r$, and inequality constraints $\sum_{i=1}^n d_i y_{i,r} \le Q$ are enforced via slack variables and a quadratic penalty term:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{H}_{\text{penalty}} = \lambda_{\text{cap}} \sum_{r=1}^{K} \left( \sum_{i=1}^{n} d_i y_{i,r} + s_r - Q \right)^2 + \lambda_{\text{visit}} \sum_{i=1}^{n} \left( \sum_{r=1}^{K} y_{i,r} - 1 \right)^2.
  \label{eq:qubo_penalty}
\end{equation}
This representation requires $\sim 1,600$ to $2,500$ logical binary variables for $n=20, K=4$. More critically, as we demonstrate below, the demand structure of \texttt{XSH-n20-k4} induces infinite-like energy barriers under single-spin and two-spin transitions.

\section{The Zero-Slack Tight-Capacity Structural Invariant}

\begin{proposition}[Zero-Slack Capacity Invariant]
Every instance in QOBLIB \texttt{09-routing} (\texttt{XSH-n20-k4-01} to \texttt{XSH-n20-k4-55}) satisfies the exact demand balance identity:
\begin{equation}
  \sum_{i=1}^{20} d_i = 4 Q \quad (\text{Total Fleet Slack } \Delta = 4Q - \sum_{i=1}^{20} d_i = 0).
  \label{eq:zero_slack}
\end{equation}
Consequently, every vehicle route $S_r \subseteq \{1,\dots,20\}$ in any feasible solution must satisfy the exact equality constraint $\sum_{i \in S_r} d_i = Q$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Suppose a feasible partition $\{S_1, S_2, S_3, S_4\}$ satisfies $\sum_{i \in S_r} d_i \le Q$ for each $r \in \{1,2,3,4\}$. Summing over all $K=4$ disjoint routes covering $\{1,\dots,20\}$ yields $\sum_{r=1}^4 \sum_{i \in S_r} d_i = \sum_{i=1}^{20} d_i = 4Q$. Since each term in the sum of 4 routes is bounded above by $Q$, equality $\sum_{i \in S_r} d_i = Q$ must hold for every $r \in \{1,2,3,4\}$. Direct inspection of all 55 \texttt{.vrp} files confirms Eq.~\eqref{eq:zero_slack} holds universally.
\end{proof}

Proposition~1 immediately explains why unconstrained penalty-based QUBO/Ising solvers (\texttt{SQA-Direct}) fail:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Absence of Single-Customer Moves:} Moving any customer $i$ with $d_i > 0$ from route $r_1$ to $r_2$ violates capacity on $r_2$ ($\sum_{j \in S_{r_2}} d_j + d_i = Q + d_i > Q$).
  \item \textbf{Rarity of Equal-Demand 2-Swaps:} Swapping $i \in S_{r_1}$ and $j \in S_{r_2}$ preserves feasibility if and only if $d_i = d_j$. For random demands in $[1, 400]$ with $Q=1000$, almost all customer pairs have $d_i \neq d_j$, creating a penalty barrier $\Delta E_{\text{pen}} = 2 \lambda_{\text{cap}} (d_i - d_j)^2 \gg \Delta E_{\text{route}}$.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Methodology}

\subsection{Algorithm 1: Tight-Capacity Bitwise Set-Partitioning (\texttt{TC-Exact})}

Instead of branching on arc variables, \texttt{TC-Exact} projects the problem onto the exact demand-tight subset manifold:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{F}_1 = \left\{ S \subseteq \{1,\dots,20\} \;\middle|\; \sum_{i \in S} d_i = Q \right\}.
\end{equation}
Representing each subset $S$ as a 20-bit integer mask $m \in [0, 2^{20}-1]$, depth-first subset-sum filtering identifies $|\mathcal{F}_1| \in [300, 961]$ valid single-vehicle masks per instance in $<0.2\text{ ms}$. For each $m \in \mathcal{F}_1$, we compute the exact optimal Traveling Salesperson Problem (TSP) tour cost $c^*(m)$ starting and ending at depot $0$ via Held-Karp dynamic programming:
\begin{equation}
  DP[A][u] = \min_{v \in A \setminus \{u\}} \left\{ DP[A \setminus \{u\}][v] + c_{vu} \right\}, \quad c^*(m) = \min_{u \in m} \left\{ DP[m][u] + c_{u0} \right\}.
\end{equation}

Finally, the 4-vehicle CVRP reduces to the Exact Cover problem over $\mathcal{F}_1$:
\begin{equation}
  \min_{m_1, m_2, m_3, m_4 \in \mathcal{F}_1} \sum_{r=1}^{4} c^*(m_r) \quad \text{s.t.} \quad m_a \wedge m_b = 0 \; (\forall a \neq b), \quad \bigvee_{r=1}^{4} m_r = 2^{20}-1.
\end{equation}
To eliminate the $4! = 24$ vehicle permutation symmetry, we anchor the lowest set bit ($\text{\texttt{\_\_builtin\_ctz}}$) of the remaining unvisited customer mask at each stage $r \in \{1,2,3\}$, and determine $m_4 = (2^{20}-1) \oplus (m_1 \vee m_2 \vee m_3)$ in $O(1)$ time via a direct lookup table $\text{cost}[m_4]$. Across all 55 instances, the total number of valid 4-way partitions $|\mathcal{P}_4|$ ranges between $372$ (\texttt{XSH-n20-k4-34}) and $5,630$ (\texttt{XSH-n20-k4-24}), enabling exhaustive global optimality certification in $\sim 3.8\text{ ms}$ per instance.

\subsection{Algorithm 2: Subspace Simulated Quantum Annealing (\texttt{SQA-Subspace})}

To evaluate quantum-inspired optimization without penalty barriers, we formulate a Path-Integral Monte Carlo Simulated Quantum Annealing (\texttt{SQA-Subspace}) solver operating directly on the feasible partition manifold $\mathcal{P}_4$. The transverse-field Hamiltonian is mapped via Suzuki-Trotter decomposition onto $P=16$ coupled classical replicas $\{\mathcal{P}^{(0)}, \dots, \mathcal{P}^{(P-1)}\}$ with effective action:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{S}_{\text{eff}}\left(\{\mathcal{P}^{(p)}\}\right) = \sum_{p=0}^{P-1} \frac{1}{P} E_{\text{CVRP}}\left(\mathcal{P}^{(p)}\right) - J_{\perp}(t) \sum_{p=0}^{P-1} \mathcal{O}\left(\mathcal{P}^{(p)}, \mathcal{P}^{(p+1 \bmod P)}\right),
\end{equation}
where $J_{\perp}(t) = -\frac{1}{2} T \ln \tanh\left(\frac{\Gamma(t)}{P T}\right)$ is the ferromagnetic imaginary-time coupling driven by transverse field $\Gamma(t)$ annealed from $\Gamma_0 = 35.0$ to $\Gamma_f = 0.175$ at fixed temperature $T = 2.5$, and $\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{P}^{(p)}, \mathcal{P}^{(q)}) = \sum_{r=1}^4 \mathbb{I}[m_r^{(p)} \in \mathcal{P}^{(q)}] \in \{0,1,2,4\}$ measures replica configuration overlap.

Quantum tunneling transitions within each Trotter slice are generated by multi-scale demand-preserving $k$-route recombination ($k \in \{2,3,4\}$): selecting $k$ routes from $\mathcal{P}^{(p)}$ with union mask $U = \bigvee_{j=1}^k m_{r_j}$ ($\sum_{i \in U} d_i = kQ$) and transitioning to a distinct $k$-tuple of disjoint masks in $\mathcal{F}_1$ whose union equals $U$.

\paragraph{Resolution of Instance \texttt{XSH-n20-k4-21}:}
In the original QOBLIB benchmark paper~\cite{koch2025qoblib}, instance \texttt{XSH-n20-k4-21} had a best reported solution of $853$, while the prior Memetic GA submission achieved $863$, and the heuristic reference listed $842$ without optimality proof. Our \texttt{TC-Exact} solver exhaustively evaluates all $|\mathcal{F}_1| = 609$ tight subsets and all $|\mathcal{P}_4| = 1,701$ feasible 4-vehicle partitions in $2.69\text{ ms}$, certifying that $\text{Cost}^* = 842$ is the unique global optimum, with a second-best partition at $\text{Cost} = 843$ (spectral gap $\Delta E = 1$). Furthermore, \texttt{SQA-Subspace} successfully tunnels out of the $\text{Cost}=843$ local minimum to reach the $\text{Cost}=842$ ground state.

\section{Implications for Quantum Hardware Implementations}

Because $|\mathcal{F}_1| \le 961 < 2^{10}$ and $|\mathcal{P}_4| \le 5,630 < 2^{13}$ across all 55 instances, our subspace reduction demonstrates that QOBLIB \texttt{09-routing} instances do not require $2,000+$ qubits on NISQ hardware. Instead, by encoding only the tight-capacity subspace (or applying constraint-preserving multi-route XY mixers in QAOA), the entire feasible space can be represented within $10$ to $13$ qubits.
